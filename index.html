What it is

In plain terms.

No background needed. This is the short version of what Constellation is and who it is for.

What it is
Software that lets an AI do a real job on your own documents and records, and hands back the result together with proof. It runs on your own computers, so your data stays with you.
How you can trust it
Nothing it says is taken on faith. Every quote and every cited record is checked by ordinary code against your original files. Anything it cannot back up is removed or held, and a person reviews and signs off.
What is built
The working platform, end to end: separate workspaces for each job, roles and sign-off, a conversation where the agent asks you questions, a log that shows any edit, and evidence reports anyone can re-verify. Its first agent reviews who accessed patient records against a hospital's own privacy policies, turning a mass of log lines into findings with their evidence attached.
Where it is going
One platform for any job that depends on documents and records. More agents are added one at a time, with the people who will use them, and each one earns trust against checks you can see.
Who it is for
Hospitals first: privacy, compliance and security teams at small and community hospitals, and the firms that serve them. The platform itself is built for any team that needs AI work it can prove.
What sets it apart
A general chatbot gives you an answer. Constellation gives you the answer, the sources, the checks and a replayable log, keeps your data on your own computers by default, and leaves the decision to a person.
How it works

From a plain request to a result a person can sign.

The agent proposes. Code and people do the rest.

  1. Ask in plain language

    The agent asks you when it is unsure, and uses the documents and records in your workspace.

  2. The agent does the job

    It works on your own hardware, so your data stays inside your environment.

  3. Code checks every claim

    Each quoted passage and each cited record entry is checked against your own files. A claim that cannot be found is marked or removed, not believed.

  4. A person decides

    Doubtful findings are held for a human. Every step is logged, and any result can be re-checked independently.

What a result carries

Built so the answer can be checked, not just trusted.

These are properties of the design. Everything measured so far was measured on synthetic data.

Its sources
Each finding points to the policy passage and the record entries it rests on, so a reviewer can open the evidence directly.
Claims checked by code
Verification is ordinary code, not a second opinion from the model. Quotes are matched word for word against your documents.
A log that shows edits
Every step goes into a hash-chained log. In the sample report it holds 18 entries and any edit, removal or reordering would show.
A fingerprint
Each report ends with a SHA-256 fingerprint, so a result can be re-verified later by someone who was not there.
Your hardware
The on-site setup makes no outbound calls. Hosted models are an explicit opt-in, off by default.
Separate workspaces
Each job keeps its own documents, records and people apart.
A named person
Results wait for sign-off from a named reviewer. The platform proposes; people decide.
The first agent

Reviewing who accessed which patient records.

The first agent we built and tested compares access to patient records against a hospital's own privacy policies. It produces an evidence report an examiner could read: each finding, the policy text it rests on, the log events behind it, what was checked and what was not, and who signed off.

The same platform is meant for other jobs as you need them, and each agent earns trust separately.

We are looking for a small or rural hospital, or a firm that serves them, to evaluate it on a de-identified sample, with scope and success measures agreed beforehand. You see exactly what it found, what it missed and why, on your own cases.
